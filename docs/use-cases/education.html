Education

Tutoring, assessment and routing decisions with a fairness requirement that is not optional.

On this page

Education decision models are judged on learning outcomes and equity, not just accuracy. A model that improves average mastery by 6 points while widening the gap for one group of students has made things worse.

Grading rubric as a decision spec

spec = DecisionSpec(
    name="short_answer_grading",
    actions=["0", "1", "2", "3", "4"],
    costs={("4","0"): 12, ("0","4"): 12,       # gross errors are worst
           ("3","2"): 2,  ("2","3"): 2,         # adjacent bands are tolerable
           ("4","3"): 1,  ("3","4"): 1},
    abstain="2", max_abstain_rate=0.20,          # 20% -> human grader
)

Adjacent-band tolerance is what makes automated grading workable: a model that is usually within one band is useful even when exact agreement is only ~70%.

Fairness controls that actually work

  • Report every metric per language, gender, and school-type slice — with confidence intervals, not point estimates.
  • Set a hard constraint: no slice may be more than 5 points below the overall within-one-band agreement.
  • Never train on demographic attributes; test for proxy leakage via a slice audit.
  • Human review of a random 5% sample, permanently — not only during the pilot.
  • Students and teachers can see and contest any automated grade.
Do not build the at-risk flag as a black box Flagging a student changes how adults treat them. Any such model needs a documented intervention path, a way to correct the record, and evidence that the intervention helps rather than stigmatises. Many teams correctly stop at "nudge the teacher with a summary" instead of "label the child".

Language coverage

Tokeniser cost is a real equity issue: the same sentence in Hindi or Tamil can cost 2–4× more tokens than in English, which shows up as higher latency and, in some products, lower quality of feedback. Measure it, then consider a multilingual SLM or a language-specific adapter rather than accepting the gap.
