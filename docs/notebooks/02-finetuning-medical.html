NB2 · Medical triage fine-tuning

A three-action triage decision under an asymmetric cost matrix — where abstention is the point, not the failure.

On this page

File: notebooks/02-finetuning-medical.ipynb · Runtime: ~6 min CPU · Data: synthetic symptom notes, 3,000 train / 1,000 test, generated in-notebook.

Not a medical device This notebook is a methodology demonstration on synthetic text. Nothing here is validated for clinical use, and no model output should ever be used for patient care without regulatory clearance and clinician oversight.

The decision

ActionMeaningCost of being wrong
routineBook a standard appointmentSevere missed case: 100 (relative units)
urgentSame-day clinician reviewOver-triage: 12
escalateImmediate human triage nurseNurse time: 4 per case

With those costs the unconstrained optimum escalates almost everything, which is exactly the trap described in decision theory. The notebook adds a capacity constraint of 30% and optimises inside it.

Expected output

policy                    accuracy  cost_per_case  abstain  coverage
argmax @ 0.5                 0.756        31.37       0.00     1.00
cost-optimal, 30% cap        1.000         8.99       0.30     0.70
cost-optimal, 10% cap        1.000        10.11       0.10     0.90

severe misses : 8.9% of true deterioration cases routed to routine
ECE           : 0.107 raw -> 0.049 calibrated (T = 0.60)
note          : the 1.000 accuracy is against the *acceptable* action set
                ({"bad": ["escalate", "urgent"], "good": ["routine", "urgent"]})

What the notebook demonstrates

  • Thresholds come from costs, not from 0.5. Moving from argmax@0.5 to the cost-optimal rule cuts cost per case by 71% — from 31.4 to 9.0.
  • The abstain cap is a business constraint. Tightening it from 30% to 10% raises cost per case from 9.0 to 10.1; that trade-off is a staffing conversation, not an ML one.
  • Accuracy needs a definition. Under a three-action cost matrix the cost-minimising action is never "urgent", so exact-match accuracy would score a sensible policy at zero. The spec's acceptable map fixes that.
  • Noise sets the floor. 20% of notes carry the other class's vocabulary, which is why severe misses settle near 9% no matter how the threshold moves.

Extending it

Swap the TF-IDF + logistic head for a fine-tuned 66M encoder (see fine-tuning) and keep everything downstream identical — same spec, same policy, same report. That swap is the whole argument for separating the decision layer from the model layer.
